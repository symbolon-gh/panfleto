## Panfleto, Calle y Memoria

**Panfleto, Calle y Memoria: Political Visual Culture in Chile, 1973–2023** is a research project and digital archive dedicated to recovering, documenting, and bringing renewed attention to the graphic expressions that have circulated through Chile’s streets over the past fifty years.

Pamphlets, leaflets, and other forms of printed street ephemera have been used to denounce injustice, call people to action, organize collective efforts, and express different positions on the country’s political and social events. Many of these materials were made to circulate quickly, be read in public spaces, and disappear soon afterward. Yet their fleeting nature does not diminish their historical or artistic value: their images, slogans, typography, colors, and physical materials bear witness to the ways different social actors have sought visibility and a voice in public life.

The collection offers a way to explore transformations in Chile’s political visual culture across three broad periods: the military dictatorship (1973–1988), the democratic transition (1989–2005), and the more recent cycle of social mobilization and change (2006–2023). These works reveal continuities, shifts, and competing visual languages that have accompanied those processes.

Today, the archive brings together **370 digitized pamphlets, 178 of which have both their front and back sides documented**. The works are organized into **19 thematic categories**, including Human Rights, Constitution, Plebiscite, Nationalist Movement, Gender, Feminism and Dissidence, Indigenous Peoples, and a range of demands concerning social rights.

Visitors can explore the collection through its contents, material characteristics, themes, and references to time and place. Where geographic information is available, individual records include the coordinates of locations mentioned or depicted in the pamphlets. These places can also be explored on the website’s **[Map]({{ '/map.html' | relative_url }})** page.

More than a collection of images from the past, this digital archive opens up new ways to observe, understand, and reconstruct collective memory through the graphic expressions that have inhabited our streets.

**Because an important part of Chile’s political and visual history was also written on small pieces of paper, once passed from hand to hand.**
